About Me

I am a Research Fellow at the University of Warwick specialising in computational materials physics. My research connects atomistic electronic structure with charge and phonon transport in semiconductors and energy-conversion materials, particularly thermoelectrics and photovoltaics. I investigate how experimentally accessible strategies, including alloying and doping, modify material properties and device performance using first-principles calculations, transport theory, spectral-function analysis, and drift–diffusion device modelling.

My current work focuses on the development of band-unfolding and spectral-function-based methods for quantifying alloy-induced electronic-structure modifications and their consequences for carrier scattering and charge transport.

Research Interests

  • Electronic structure and carrier transport
  • Semiconductor physics and devices
  • Thermoelectric materials and half-Heusler alloys
  • Photovoltaic materials and drift–diffusion device modelling
  • Alloy disorder, band unfolding, and spectral-function analysis
  • Electron–phonon interactions and carrier-scattering mechanisms
  • Phonon transport and anharmonic lattice dynamics
  • Low-dimensional and topological materials
  • Machine-learned interatomic potentials and Hamiltonians
